## The headline finding

The input research document is built on a boom narrative that the 2026 data contradicts.

Hyderabad carries a **19-month residential inventory overhang — the worst of any major Indian city**
(national average ~14 months; Bengaluru 8). Roughly **97,000 unsold units** sit in the market. Neopolis in
Kokapet is on ~40% unsold stock with price cuts. Sales grew **1%** in H1 2026 while launches ran ahead of
absorption. Average price is **₹8,258/sq ft**, not the ₹9,430 claimed.

Meanwhile the demand engine is genuinely national-best: GCCs took **3.4 mn sq ft — 45% of all office
leasing** in H1 2026, and Hyderabad is India's **#1 GCC destination**.

This is not a demand collapse. It is a **distribution and absorption failure** — which is a better setup
for OTHO than the boom narrative, not a worse one:

- **Developers are the desperate party.** Capital is locked in unsold towers. They need *clearance*, not
  leads. That is real pricing power on exclusive mandates.
- **Buyers face paralysis, not scarcity.** With ~97,000 options, the product is not "find me a property"
  — it is *"tell me which of these will not destroy my capital."* Honest Negatives, True Density, Loading
  Factor and UDS are the core utility in a glut, not ethical garnish.

**North Star metric:** Inventory Clearance Velocity — months to clear mandate inventory versus the
19-month market baseline.
