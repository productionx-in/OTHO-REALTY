Two things are true at once. The category is large and growing fast. And in Hyderabad specifically, developers are sitting on stock they cannot move — agents are quoting Neopolis units near ₹6,300/sq ft against list prices of ₹11,000–12,000.

That gap is the opening. When a developer's own pricing is being undercut by half in the grey market, the thing they need is not another listings portal. It is someone who can bring them buyers who arrive already convinced.

03

The gap

Every major Indian property platform is built on the same business: collect a lead, sell it to several brokers, let them fight over it. The buyer pays for that with their phone.

What buyers actually complain about

  • Spam is complaint number one across user reviews of every major portal
  • People keep a second "property phone" — or change their number entirely
  • 43% report encountering fraudulent listings
  • Listings cloned from other portals; AI-generated fake interiors
  • Same flat listed by eight brokers at six prices
Source · user review analysis, industry reporting 2026

Why nobody fixes it

They can't. Reselling one lead to eight brokers is the revenue model. A portal that promised never to share your number would have to dismantle its own P&L to keep the promise.

That's the gap. It isn't a feature they forgot to build — it's a door their business model locks from the inside.

A platform with two million listings is worthless if enquiring on one gets you eight calls.

Meanwhile the thing buyers most want help with — which of these should I actually buy? — is not served by anyone. Portals answer "what is available." Nobody answers "what is good."

Square Yards built ₹1,410cr of revenue and kept ₹46cr of it. Volume brokerage is a treadmill. OTHO should never compete on listing count or transaction volume — that race is won by whoever burns the most money, and the prize is a 3% margin.

05

The model: CarWale, for property

There's a company that already solved this exact problem in a neighbouring category, and it's worth copying deliberately.

CarWale did not beat the classifieds by having more cars. It won by owning the step before the transaction — expert reviews, side-by-side comparisons, price guides, owner ratings, EMI calculators. It became where Indians go to decide. It reached 65 million car buyers and built roughly $144M of revenue on the intent it had earned.

Classifieds business

Answers "what's available." Monetises by reselling your attention to whoever pays. Wins on volume and ad spend. Buyer is the product.

Research business

Answers "what should I buy." Monetises the intent it has already earned. Wins on trust and depth. Buyer is the customer.

In Indian real estate the research layer is empty. No Zestimate, no expert project reviews, no honest comparison engine, no resident ratings. Every player is fighting over the classifieds half.

Own the decision, and the transaction follows you.

This also happens to be exactly what OTHO already is. The founders' advantage is judgement built over twenty years. A research platform is simply that judgement, made public and made searchable.

How we say it in one line

"Every property platform tells you what's for sale. OTHO tells you what's worth buying."

Built in four layers

Layer 1Days 1–90
Trust
Website, project pages, the comparison engine, reviews and guides. No listings, no transactions. We earn the right to be believed before we ask for anything.
Layer 2Months 4–12
Coverage
Every meaningful Hyderabad project reviewed and comparable. Resident reviews live. Price estimates from registration data. OTHO becomes the default place to check before buying in this city.
Layer 3Year 2
Transactions
Exclusive developer mandates, site visits, bookings. Revenue arrives on intent we already own, so cost per booking is a fraction of what portal leads cost.
Layer 4Year 3+
Services and data
Loans, legal, registration, interiors. And the data business — developers and banks paying for what OTHO knows about demand. This is the high-margin end.

The sequencing is the strategy

Every competitor started at Layer 3 and tried to reverse into trust. It doesn't work — you can't earn credibility from people already annoyed by your calls. Starting at Layer 1 is slower for six months and much harder to copy afterwards.

Where this goes

The founders' ambition is national and then global. Here is what that looks like as a sequence rather than a slogan.

Year 1Hyderabad west
Own one micro-market completely
Kokapet, Financial District, Narsingi, Tellapur. Every project reviewed, compared and honestly rated. Revenue from mandates. The goal is not scale — it's proof that the model converts better than portal leads.
Year 2Hyderabad
The whole city, plus transactions
Full city coverage. Exclusive mandates as the core revenue line. Services layer live. OTHO becomes the name buyers check before they visit anything.
Years 3–4South India
Bangalore, Chennai, Pune
The content and review system is city-agnostic — only the data changes. Each new city launches on the template Neopolis produced, with a local team and the same standard of honesty.
Years 5–7India
National platform, data business
Top ten cities. The high-margin line arrives: developers, banks and funds paying for OTHO's demand data. This is where PropEquity earns ~45% EBITDA, and where OTHO's coverage becomes genuinely valuable.
Years 8–10Global
NRI corridors first
The natural first step abroad is not a new market — it's the same market from the other side. Gulf, Singapore, US and UK buyers purchasing Indian property, who need trustworthy remote due diligence more than anyone. Then the model travels to comparable markets.
Ten years is four cities and one honest habit, repeated.

What makes it defensible

A competitor can copy the website in a quarter. What they cannot copy is a library of hundreds of honest project reviews built over years, the resident reviews attached to them, and a reputation for saying what's wrong with a building. That archive compounds and can't be bought.
