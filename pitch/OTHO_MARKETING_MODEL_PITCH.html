01 · The opportunity

The market is oversupplied. That is the whole opportunity.

Hyderabad is not short of property. It is short of judgement. That single fact determines the model.

But the demand engine is genuinely national-best: GCCs took 3.4 mn sq ft — 45% of all office leasing in H1 2026, and Hyderabad is India's #1 GCC destination. Jobs and incomes are being created. They are simply not converting into transactions.

This is not a demand collapse. It is a distribution failure.

Buyers cannot tell 97,000 options apart, and the channel that is supposed to help them is optimised for spam. That produces the two commercial facts this entire model rests on.

Fact 1 — Developers are the desperate party

Their capital is locked in unsold towers. Every month of overhang is carrying cost they cannot recover.

They do not need more leads. They need clearance. A firm that can prove it clears faster than the 19-month baseline has real pricing power on exclusive mandates — 2.5–4% against a 1–3% standard channel rate.

Fact 2 — Buyers face paralysis, not scarcity

With 97,000 options, "help me find a property" is worthless. "Tell me which of these will not destroy my capital" is the product.

Loading factor, UDS, true density and honest negatives aren't ethics. In a glut they are the core utility — and nobody in India publishes them.

The window is open now

Portals cannot follow us. Their revenue is reselling your phone number to eight brokers — they cannot promise not to without killing their own P&L.

Meanwhile TRAI and DPDP enforcement is making that model legally expensive. We arrive exactly as the incumbent model starts failing publicly.

02 · How we initiate

Authority first. Inventory second. Platform third.

We do not open by asking developers for exclusivity. We open by publishing the number that quantifies their problem — and let them call us.

The move that starts everything: The Hyderabad Inventory Report

A free quarterly research report on inventory overhang by micro-market. Unsold units by area and price band, months-of-overhang, launch-vs-absorption, price per carpet foot (not per SBA foot — that alone is newsworthy), and a possession-delay index computed from TG-RERA filings.

It inverts the sales conversation

We never cold-pitch. We publish "Neopolis is at 40% unsold and 26 months of overhang" — and the Neopolis developers need to talk to us. The report is the business development engine.

It manufactures press

Financial journalists need hyper-local numbers and have none. Tier-1 backlinks that no budget buys — and the foundation of both search and AI citation.

It is the trust proof

Publishing an unflattering number about the market you earn from is the costly signal that makes "radical transparency" believable rather than a slogan.

It is the future data product

Phase 2's paid B2B subscription is this exact report, productised. We build the asset while using it as marketing.

Then one deliberately uncomfortable article

"We could earn commission on 14 projects in Kokapet. We're recommending 8. Here's the arithmetic on the other 6."

Grounded only in computed, defensible metrics — loading above the 25–35% norm without amenity justification, density above tolerance, UDS below peer set, possession slippage against TG-RERA dates. This single piece does more for trust than a year of advertising, because it is verifiably against our own short-term interest.

Mandate acquisition — sequenced, never sprayed

The instinct is to chase 10–15 mandates immediately. That produces ten thin, under-resourced campaigns and no proof point. The sequence matters more than the count.

Google — and the campaign nobody else can run

Standard campaigns bid on desire: 4 BHK Kokapet, luxury apartments Financial District. Every developer bids there, CPCs run ₹150–400, and we will compete there too.

But nobody bids on doubt.

[project] review · [project] problems · Kokapet traffic · [area] waterlogging · [A] vs [B] · [project] possession delay

Near-zero competition, very high intent, low CPC — and we are the only advertiser in the market with honest content to land it on. A developer cannot bid on "Project X problems" and send you to a page that answers it. This is structural, not tactical. It is the first campaign we build, and we expect it to be our cheapest quality source at ≤₹500 CPL.

The second Google lever is Quality Score — worth a 20–40% cost advantage on the identical keyword. Ours is structural too: a comparison result and a dictionary page are genuinely the most relevant possible destinations for comparison and definitional queries. A developer microsite cannot match that relevance, because it only ever has one answer.

Meta — visual storytelling, then relentless retargeting

Real estate is visual and Meta is where the household is. But Meta leads convert far worse than search leads, so we optimise it on cost per qualified lead, never raw CPL. Optimising Meta on raw CPL is the single most expensive mistake in Indian real estate marketing — it trains the algorithm to find people who click, not people who buy.

Top — demand creation

Micro-market documentaries: "We drove Kokapet to HITEC City at 9am. Here's the real number." Honest-negative clips. Podcast cuts. No lead-gen objectives here — ThruPlay and qualified traffic only.

Middle — tools that qualify

Comparison carousels with real numbers. The Loading Factor Calculator. The Inventory Report. Someone who completes a UDS calculation is a fundamentally different prospect from someone who taps "Get Price."

Bottom — dynamic retargeting

Keyed to the exact comparison you viewed, 30-day window. IT professionals in the Gachibowli corridor. NRIs in the US, UAE, UK and Singapore — on FEMA-safety creative, not lifestyle, because the remote buyer's dominant emotion is fear of being cheated at distance.

Two technical requirements that are not optional: server-side Conversions API with hashed CRM identifiers, and offline conversion upload — pushing site-visit and booking events back from CRM into Google and Meta. That is the difference between an algorithm optimising for form-fills and one optimising for actual buyers. It is the highest-ROI technical task in the entire plan.

The way in is exactly the content we were already going to build: direct definitional answers, explicit formulae, sourced original data. AI engines quote precision and skip prose. And original data gets cited — aggregated commentary does not. Our quarterly report is the citation engine.

WhatsApp — and a cost lever worth knowing

Marketing messages cost ₹0.86 + 18% GST = ₹1.02. Utility messages cost ₹0.115 — and are free inside the 24-hour customer service window until 1 Oct 2026.

So we architect flows where the customer opens the window via click-to-WhatsApp, then run all qualification and nurture as utility messages inside it. Roughly an 8× per-message cost difference — and it converts better, because they initiated.

How Prop-Talk actually gets made

Most branded podcasts die at episode 8, because they are scheduled weekly and produced one at a time. We design against that failure mode from the start.

Batch production

Four episodes in a single studio day, once a quarter. The only model that survives contact with founder calendars. One day buys a whole quarter of content.

Guest ladder — hardest last

Episodes 1–6: practitioners — RERA lawyers, structural engineers, urban planners, valuers. They say yes immediately and their content is evergreen.
7–14: analysts, CREDAI, PropTech founders.
15+: developers. Not before.

Why developers come last

Opening with developers and ministers inverts the difficulty curve — and makes the show look like paid advertorial from episode one. We earn the access rather than leading with it.

One day → ~50 assets

Per episode: long-form video, audio, 6–10 vertical clips, an SEO article from the transcript, a LinkedIn carousel, quote graphics, a newsletter section, 2–3 Meta creatives.

This is the economics that make the whole content plan affordable.

Honest Negatives — the charter that makes it shippable

This is our biggest differentiator and our biggest execution risk. We earn from developers while publishing unflattering facts about their locations. That needs structure, not courage.

We publish only these threeExampleLegal exposure
Civic & environmental — public record"TG PCB station recorded PM2.5 of N µg/m³, Nov–Jan average"Low — factual, attributed
Computed structural — arithmetic from filings"Loading factor 41% against a 25–35% norm"Low — maths from TG-RERA

"This project has a 41% loading factor against a 25–35% norm" is a defensible arithmetic statement.
"This builder cheats you on area" is litigable.

We publish only the first kind — and to an analytical buyer the first kind is more persuasive anyway. Never subjective quality judgements, never allegations about developer character, never unverified forum claims.

Three rules that keep it credible

  • Right of reply, never right of veto. 7 days' notice, unedited developer response published alongside.
  • Negatives apply to our own mandates too. Non-negotiable. Exempt our own inventory and the whole asset is worthless — buyers will notice within a quarter. KPI: ≥1 published negative per mandate.
  • Always pair a negative with mitigation. "Kokapet has peak congestion on the ORR approach and localised monsoon waterlogging. Eastern-edge towers with the elevated approach are materially less affected — commute before 08:30 and the delta is ~18 minutes." That is what converts a transparency reader into a lead.

And how we sell it to the developer

The negatives are already public — on Reddit, on X, in society WhatsApp groups, in Google reviews. The choice was never published-vs-unpublished. It is contextualised with mitigation, or uncontrolled. And a developer sitting at 28% loading and 88 units/acre gains enormously when those become standard comparison fields. Transparency is a weapon for the well-built project. We lead the mandate pitch with that, and the objection becomes the selling point.

Loading Factor

The gap between the area you are sold and the area you can actually use, as a percentage of carpet area. A 1,000 sq ft flat marketed as 1,400 sq ft carries a 40% loading factor.

Loading Factor = (SBA − Carpet) ÷ Carpet × 100

Is 31% normal? Yes. Standard apartments run 25–35%. Large integrated townships with central parks and sports facilities can legitimately reach 35–45% for genuinely shared amenities. Above 45% without a matching amenity footprint, ask what you are buying.

Note that we publish the defence of high loading too. That nuance is what makes the metric credible rather than alarmist — and it protects us from the charge that we simply attack projects we don't represent.

What we measure — and what we deliberately ignore

The North Star

Inventory Clearance Velocity — months to clear mandate inventory vs the 19-month market baseline.

Year 1 target ≤13 months (32% better than market). Year 2 ≤10 months. It prices our mandates, it's externally benchmarkable, and it cannot be faked — you cannot invent a sold unit.

What we hold weekly

Cost per qualified lead ≤₹3,000 · cost per site visit ≤₹11,000 · cost per booking ≤₹70,000 · ROAS ≥6:1 on mandate campaigns · speed-to-lead <5 min median · lead leakage <2% · leads shared with third parties: 0%, permanently.

What we refuse to optimise

Raw lead volume — that's the classifieds metric; chasing it rebuilds what we exist to replace. Raw CPL — Meta will happily sell ₹200 leads that never answer. Page views, followers, app downloads. And GMV — the Square Yards trap: ₹1,410cr revenue on 3.3% EBITDA.

Attribution built for a 9-month cycle

Cohort by first-touch month, then track to booking — monthly CPA snapshots actively mislead here and will make us kill the campaigns that are working. Plus self-reported attribution on every form, to catch the podcast and dark-social contribution no pixel sees.

The cash-flow point the room should hear. Commission is paid on registration, not booking — a 3–6 month lag. So revenue trails bookings by roughly two quarters, and peak cash need (₹6–7cr) is materially higher than the net 18-month position suggests. Two mitigations go into every mandate contract: milestone-based commission release, and developer-funded campaign media so we are not fronting ad spend against back-ended income.

Three roles that decide whether this works

Data Engineer Hire first

The comparison engine is a data acquisition and verification problem, not a UI problem — TG-RERA filings, HMDA approvals, IGRS records, brochures, physical audit, all into one schema. This is where PropTech companies fail. Underhire here and nothing else in the plan matters.

Sales Head

Owns Inventory Clearance Velocity — the North Star. Not a lead-volume manager: a conversion-rate owner. The entire unit economic model rests on beating portal conversion benchmarks, and that is this person's mandate.

Research / Data Lead

Produces the quarterly Inventory Report — which is simultaneously our business development engine, our PR engine, our AI-citation engine and our future B2B product. One person, four jobs, and probably the highest-leverage hire in the company.

What we deliberately do not hire

No in-house creative studio (agency + the podcast producer covers it) · no separate PR agency (the report is the PR) · no full-time legal (retained counsel) · and no lead-generation team — the moment we staff for lead volume rather than lead quality, we have become the thing we set out to replace.

The discipline that protects the margin

Refuse any mandate below 55% contribution margin

Square Yards built ₹1,410cr of revenue and kept ₹46cr of it. Volume in Indian PropTech is a trap. We would rather run 10 profitable mandates than 30 that flatter the GMV slide.

Two P&Ls, never blended

Mandate media is developer-funded and judged on ROAS. Brand media is ours and judged on CAC. Mixing them makes both numbers meaningless — and is how agencies hide underperformance.

Concentration cap

No single developer above 35% of mandate GMV in Phase 1, 25% in Phase 2. One relationship souring should never be an existential event.

The comparables

Anarock: ₹946cr FY26, +25%, advisory + mandates. PropEquity: ₹50.7cr at ~45% EBITDA — data is high-margin but small. We are building Anarock's revenue architecture with PropEquity's margin layer attached.
