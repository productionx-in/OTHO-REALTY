The problem marketing actually owns

The wedge

There is one fact in the public record I want to build the whole plan around, because it is a marketing problem rather than a sales problem, and it is the clearest case for funding this work.

Neopolis inventory is being quoted by agents at around ₹6,300/sq ft against official pricing of ₹11,000–12,000/sq ft Sourced — a gap of nearly half, reported in the trade press alongside roughly 40% of some 10,000 launched homes unsold across the layout.

When a project's price is set in the grey market by whoever is most desperate to move a unit, three things happen, and all three are brand damage:

  • The advertised price stops being believed. Every serious buyer now assumes the real number is lower and opens by asking for the discount.
  • The discounter sets your positioning. Whoever quotes lowest defines the project in the buyer's mind, and it is never the developer.
  • Honest buyers feel punished. Anyone who paid list price learns they overpaid, and they say so publicly.
Discounting is what happens when demand is not being generated. The marketing answer is enough direct, attributable demand that you are never negotiating from a position of needing this month's booking.

That reframing is the reason to run marketing properly rather than buy portal leads: it is the difference between paying for access to buyers and owning the relationship with them.

The part I need you to check

The pricing gap above comes from trade reporting on the Neopolis micro-market generally, not from your books. You will know whether it reflects what is actually happening with your inventory. If it does not, tell me — the diagnosis changes and so does the plan.

What I decide, and what I bring to you

You have run this business for twenty years. You should not be approving ad copy variations, and I should not be repositioning the project on my own judgement. The line I would draw:

I run without asking

  • Creative variations within approved messaging
  • Budget shifts between channels inside the agreed total
  • Targeting, bidding and audience changes
  • Publishing schedule and content topics
  • Pausing anything underperforming

I bring to you first

  • Anything that changes price or offer
  • New positioning or a change to core messaging
  • Total budget changes
  • Anything naming a competitor
  • Public response to a complaint or negative press
  • Any claim about delivery dates or approvals

Alongside that, weekly written content answers the questions buyers are already searching — how loading factor changes the real price per usable foot, what UDS means for long-term value, how Kokapet compares to the alternatives on the specifics rather than the adjectives.

Answering the awkward questions honestly is the cheapest trust you can buy, and in a market with this much unsold stock it is also the most persuasive.

Staying inside the rules

Compliance

You will have seen enforcement tighten. I am raising it here because it constrains channel design from day one, not because it is a formality — and because getting it wrong is expensive in a way that is visible to your buyers.

RegimeWhat it requiresExposure
TG‑RERA Sourced Registration number in every advertisement, across print, digital and outdoor. Projects registered before any marketing begins. TG‑RERA has issued notices to developers for misleading and pre-launch advertising and levied roughly ₹10cr in penalties.
TRAI TCCCPR Sourced Commercial calls only between 9am and 9pm. Consent for commercial contact is short-lived and must be current. Complaint windows are now days, not weeks. ₹1 lakh first violation, ₹2 lakh subsequent. Enforcement at scale against telemarketers through 2025.
DPDP Act & Rules Sourced Consent must be specific, informed and separate from terms and conditions. Withdrawal as easy as granting. Core obligations phase in through the notified runway. Building it in now costs nothing; retrofitting later costs a rebuild.

Practically, this means the lead capture, the calling window, the WhatsApp opt-in and the ad templates all get designed once, correctly, in the first three weeks. It is unglamorous work and it is why the launch phase starts at day 31 rather than day 3.

Making it repeatable

Project two

The reason to accept a slower first 90 days is that most of the work is built once and reused. When the next project starts, this is what already exists:

Inherited, near-zero setup

  • Tracking and attribution architecture
  • CRM configuration, routing and SLA
  • Consent and compliance templates
  • Ad account structure and audiences
  • Reporting formats and review rhythm
  • Channel partner kit structure
  • Production workflow and shoot checklist
  • Content formats proven to work

Rebuilt per project

  • Positioning and messaging
  • Creative assets and shoot
  • Microsite content
  • Locality and comparison content
  • Budget and channel weighting
  • Audience definition

By my estimate, project two reaches launch in roughly a third of the time. That estimate is judgement from the structure of the work, not a measured figure We measure — and Neopolis is what will tell us the real number.
