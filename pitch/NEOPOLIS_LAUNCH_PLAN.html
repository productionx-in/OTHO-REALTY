When agents quote half the list price, the project's price gets set by whoever needs the sale most that week. Buyers stop believing the advertised number. Anyone who paid full price feels cheated.

Discounting is what fills the gap when demand isn't being created. Our job is to create enough of it that OTHO isn't negotiating from a weak position.

03

The approach

Big developers will always outspend you on advertising. So we don't compete there.

My Home and Brigade can put ₹50 lakh a month into Google and Meta. If OTHO tries to win that fight, it loses slowly and expensively. The channel where money doesn't decide the winner is content — and that happens to be where OTHO's real advantage sits.

What big budgets buy

Attention, rented by the day. Stop paying and it disappears the same afternoon. Every competitor can buy the same thing.

What content builds

Trust, owned permanently. An article that ranks brings enquiries for years. Two decades of market knowledge can't be bought by anyone else.

So the order is deliberate. Build OTHO into a name people trust first. Sell Neopolis through that trust second. Advertising comes in as an amplifier once there's something worth amplifying.

This also happens to be the only approach that works at the budget we're proposing. At ₹1 lakh of media a month you cannot buy your way to volume — but you can absolutely publish your way to authority.

Why this suits OTHO specifically

Every Hyderabad property channel we found is run by a broker or a builder selling their own stock — Raja Reddy's channel, RealTalks Hyderabad, PropGo, GPR Realtors. Useful content, but everyone knows what they're there to sell.

Nobody is doing the advisory voice: someone who'll say what's wrong with a project. That slot is open, and it's exactly where OTHO's positioning already sits.

04

Building the OTHO name

Three things, running together. All of them cheap to produce and impossible for a competitor to copy.

The podcast

A Hyderabad property conversation, on camera, fortnightly. Thirty to forty minutes with one guest. This is the single highest-value thing OTHO can do, for one reason: the founders can get people on camera that nobody else can. Twenty years of relationships with developers, architects and planners is the whole moat, and a podcast is how it becomes public.

Production X shoots and edits it. We already own the cameras, so this costs a fraction of what an agency would quote.

The blog

Four to six articles a month, most of them pulled straight from podcast material rather than written from scratch. Five topics we'd keep returning to:

Plain definitions

Loading factor, carpet versus super built-up, UDS, RERA terms. Simple explanations of things buyers are too embarrassed to ask about.

Locality guides

Kokapet, Financial District, Narsingi, Tellapur, Mokila. What each is actually like, including the drawbacks.

Monthly market note

Registrations, prices, launches. Real numbers, published on a schedule. This is what gets OTHO quoted by journalists.

Project comparisons

Honest side-by-sides including what's weak. The most-read and most-shared format in this category.

Buyer guides

Home loans, registration, stamp duty, tax. Boring, constantly searched, and almost nobody explains it well.

Answering AI

Written so ChatGPT and Gemini quote OTHO when someone asks about Kokapet. Increasingly the first place buyers ask.

The founders' own voice

Two or three LinkedIn posts a week from the founders. Not company announcements — observations. What they saw at a site this week, why a project is priced oddly, what a developer told them.

Twenty years of stories is content nobody can replicate, and it costs nothing but fifteen minutes. We'll draft and they approve, or they voice-note and we write it up.

What this looks like after six months

Around 12 podcast episodes, 30 articles, 100+ clips, and a founder with a real following. Search and AI start sending enquiries that cost nothing per lead — and that share keeps growing every month after.

Six rules we build to

Phone first

Most traffic arrives on a phone, often on weak signal. Designed for that screen and tested on real 4G.

Nothing behind a form

Price, plans and location visible without giving a number. Gating loses more buyers than it captures.

Under three seconds

Every second of delay costs enquiries. Page weight watched weekly.

One action per screen

Book a visit, download the plan, or start a WhatsApp chat. Never all three at once.

Written for a nervous buyer

Someone spending three crore wants reasons to trust you. Straight answers beat adjectives.

RERA number everywhere

Required in all advertising. Built into the template so it can't be forgotten.

The one that costs nothing and matters most

Response time. A lead that waits four hours is usually gone. We put a response clock in the CRM from day one and report it weekly. It's free, and it protects every rupee spent upstream.
