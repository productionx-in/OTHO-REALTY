## 0. The thesis in one page

The input research document argues that Hyderabad is booming and OTHO should build a platform to capture the
boom. **The 2026 data says otherwise, and the correction is good news.**

Hyderabad carries a **19-month residential inventory overhang — the worst of any major Indian city**
(national average ~14 months; Bengaluru 8). Roughly **97,000 unsold units** sit in the market. Neopolis in
Kokapet — the "Golden Mile" the input document holds up as the top-performing micro-market — is sitting on
**~40% unsold stock with price cuts**. Sales grew **1%** in H1 2026 while launches ran at 20,466 units
against 19,249 sold. Meanwhile the demand engine is genuinely national-best: GCCs took **3.4 mn sq ft, 45%
of all office leasing** in H1 2026, and Hyderabad is India's **#1 GCC destination**.

So this is not a demand collapse. It is a **distribution and absorption failure**. Jobs and incomes are
being created faster than the residential market can convert them into transactions, because buyers cannot
tell the 97,000 options apart and the channel that is supposed to help them is optimised for spam.

That produces the two commercial facts OTHO's entire strategy should rest on:

> **Fact 1 — Developers are the desperate party.** Their capital is locked in unsold towers. What they need
> is not leads. It is **clearance**. An advisory firm that can prove it clears inventory faster than the
> 19-month market baseline has genuine pricing power on exclusive mandates.
>
> **Fact 2 — Buyers face paralysis, not scarcity.** With ~97,000 options, "help me find a property" is
> worthless. **"Tell me which of these will not destroy my capital"** is the product. Honest Negatives,
> True Density, Loading Factor and UDS are not ethical garnish — in a glut, they are the core utility.

Everything downstream follows from this inversion. OTHO does not position as a better portal. It positions
as **the inventory clearance and capital-protection layer** that sits above a broken market.

**The one-line positioning:**

> **OTHO tells you what the brochure won't — and what the broker can't afford to.**

**What OTHO must refuse to do:** chase GMV. Square Yards built **₹1,410cr of revenue on ₹46cr of EBITDA —
a 3.3% margin**. Volume brokerage in India is a low-margin trap. Anarock (₹946cr FY26, +25%) and PropEquity
(₹50.7cr at ~45% EBITDA) show where margin actually lives: **exclusive mandates and proprietary data**.

### The strategic conclusion the SWOT forces

OTHO's defensible moat is **not the platform**. Features get copied. The moat is the compound of:
**(a) exclusive inventory** competitors literally cannot list, **(b) a verified proprietary dataset** built
through physical audit, and **(c) a trust brand** that legacy portals cannot adopt without cannibalising
their own revenue model. The platform is the *delivery mechanism* for those three. Build in that order.

## 2. Section 1 — Initiation & Launch Strategy

**Objective:** establish presence, manufacture trust before the platform exists, and convert the founders'
30-year relationships into **10–15 exclusive mandates within 18 months**.

**The sequencing principle:** *authority before inventory, inventory before platform.* OTHO must be
publicly useful before it asks a developer for exclusivity, because the pitch is not "we're a good broker"
— it is "we are the market's analyst, and analysts get exclusivity."

### Phase 0 — Foundation & Legal Armour (Days 0–90)

Nothing customer-facing ships. This phase exists to make everything after it defensible.

**0.1 Regulatory foundation (Week 1–3) — blocking, do not proceed without it**

| Action | Detail |
|---|---|
| TG-RERA agent registration | Mandatory. 5-year validity. Non-registration penalty **₹10,000/day** or up to **5% of facilitated project cost**. |
| Advertising compliance SOP | RERA registration number in **every** advertisement — search, social, display, outdoor, brochure, landing page footer. TG-RERA is actively penalising violations. Build the number into ad templates so it cannot be omitted. |
| TRAI telemarketing registration | Registered-telemarketer status; calling window hard-coded **09:00–21:00**; consent validity treated as **7 days** for commercial transactions. |
| DPDP readiness | Standalone plain-language consent notice, separate from T&Cs. Purpose-specific consent. Consent dashboard with one-click withdrawal. Full obligations due ~mid-May 2027 — build compliant now rather than retrofit. |
| Legal review of Honest Negatives | Retain a media/defamation counsel to sign off the editorial standard **before** the first negative is published. |

> **Reframe this as marketing, not overhead.** "OTHO is a TG-RERA registered advisor (Reg. No. XXXX) that
> has never resold a customer's phone number" is a *differentiating claim*, and by 2027 most of the
> competition will be scrambling to make it.

**0.2 The data spine (Week 2–10)**

Start the dataset before the website. The data is the product; the site is a viewer.

- Choose **40–60 projects** across the six priority micro-markets (Kokapet/Neopolis, Financial District/
  Gachibowli, Narsingi, Tellapur/Gopanpally, Kollur/Mokila, Miyapur/Bachupally).
- For each, capture the **OTHO Verified Record**: TG-RERA registration number and filing history, sanctioned
  plan, total plot area, unit count, tower/floor configuration, RERA carpet area per unit type, SBA per unit
  type, **computed loading factor**, **computed UDS**, **computed true density (units/acre)**, possession
  timeline vs original RERA-committed date, and completion status.
- Do the physical audit: site visit, photographs, traffic timing at peak, monsoon drainage observation,
  approach road width, distance-to-employment drive test at 9am and 6pm.
- **This is the asset.** It is why OTHO can publish comparisons no portal can, and why developers will
  eventually pay for the B2B data product.

**0.3 Brand & identity (Week 4–12)**

- Positioning line locked: *"OTHO tells you what the brochure won't."*
- Visual system: deliberately analytical, not aspirational. Every competitor uses golden-hour drone footage
  of infinity pools. OTHO should look like a research house — data-forward, restrained, credible. **The
  visual differentiation carries the positioning before a single word is read.**
- Founder narrative: 30 years, X transactions, Y families, Z developers. Specific numbers, not adjectives.

**Phase 0 exit criteria:** RERA registered · 50+ projects fully audited · legal sign-off on the negatives
standard · brand system locked · CRM instance live with the no-resale architecture configured.

### Phase 1 — Manufactured Authority (Days 91–270)

The core play. OTHO earns the right to ask for mandates by becoming the market's analyst first.

**1.1 The Trojan horse: *The Hyderabad Inventory Report*** ← *the single highest-leverage launch action*

Publish a quarterly, free, rigorously sourced report on **inventory overhang by micro-market**. Not a
listings site. A research artefact — the thing Anarock and Knight Frank do, at hyper-local resolution they
do not bother with.

Contents: unsold units by micro-market and by price band, months-of-overhang per micro-market, launch vs
absorption trend, price-per-carpet-foot (not per SBA foot — this alone is newsworthy), possession-delay
index computed from TG-RERA filings.

Why this is the right first move:

1. **It inverts the mandate conversation.** OTHO does not cold-pitch developers. It publishes the number
   that quantifies their pain, and their sales heads call. A firm that publishes "Neopolis is at 40% unsold
   and 26 months of overhang" is a firm the Neopolis developers need to talk to.
2. **It is media-ready.** Financial journalists need hyper-local numbers and have none. This generates
   tier-one backlinks (Economic Times, Moneycontrol, Telangana Today, Deccan Chronicle) that no amount of
   link-building buys — and those backlinks are the foundation of both classical SEO and AI citation.
3. **It is the trust proof.** Publishing an unflattering number about the market OTHO earns from is the
   costly signal that makes "radical transparency" believable.
4. **It is the B2B SaaS product's MVP.** The Phase 2 subscription business is this report, productised.

**Cadence:** quarterly, with a monthly one-page registration-data update. First edition ships Day ~120.

**1.2 The trust proof act — *The Kokapet Report***

Alongside the market report, publish one deliberately uncomfortable piece:

> *"We could earn commission on 14 projects in Kokapet. We're recommending 8. Here's the arithmetic on the
> other 6."*

Grounded strictly in computed, defensible metrics: loading factor above the 25–35% norm without amenity
justification, true density above tolerance, UDS materially below peer set, possession slippage against
TG-RERA-committed dates.

This single article does more for brand trust than a year of advertising, because it is **verifiably against
OTHO's own short-term interest**. Seed it to the r/Hyderabad and r/india communities, IT-company internal
forums, and NRI Telegram/WhatsApp investment groups — audiences that are hostile to marketing and receptive
to arithmetic.

**1.3 Prop-Talk launch (Day ~150)** — see §4 for production detail.

**1.4 Mandate acquisition — sequenced, not sprayed**

Do **not** chase 10–15 mandates at once. The sequence matters more than the count.

| Step | Target | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchor mandate** (Month 4–6) | **One** Tier-1 developer, on a **stuck** project — high unsold %, past its launch momentum, in a micro-market OTHO knows cold | A stuck project has low opportunity cost for the developer, so exclusivity is cheap to grant. And clearance on a stuck project is the most persuasive case study obtainable |
| **Proof** (Month 6–10) | Clear inventory measurably faster than the 19-month market baseline | This number *is* the sales deck for every subsequent mandate |
| **Expansion** (Month 8–14) | 4–6 mandates, same developers, better projects | Land-and-expand inside existing relationships beats new logos |
| **Scale** (Month 12–18) | 10–15 total, across 4+ developer groups | Concentration risk: cap any single developer at <35% of mandate GMV |

**Commercial terms to negotiate:**

- **Commission 2.5–4%** vs the 1–3% standard channel-partner rate (up to 5% luxury). Justified by
  exclusivity, full-funnel ownership and technology.
- **Developer-funded media at OTHO's discretion.** Critical structural point: this is how Anarock and Square
  Yards scale without burning their own balance sheet. It also creates **two separate P&Ls** — mandate media
  (developer-funded, governed by ROAS) and platform brand media (OTHO-funded, governed by CAC). Never
  co-mingle them in reporting.
- **Performance kicker** above an agreed clearance velocity — aligns incentives and prices OTHO's edge.
- **Data rights clause** — OTHO retains rights to anonymised transaction and absorption data. This is what
  builds the Phase 2 B2B asset. Negotiate it in Mandate #1, when nobody is thinking about it yet.
- **Right of reply, not right of veto** on Honest Negatives (see §3.3). Put this in the mandate contract
  from day one so it is a known term, never a later fight.

**1.5 The NRI beachhead (Month 6+)**

NRIs are the highest-ticket, most under-served and most trust-dependent segment — a remote buyer literally
cannot verify anything and must rely on a proxy. Launch the FEMA/repatriation content set, a US/UAE/UK/
Singapore-timed video-call advisory desk, and a "we walked the site for you" verified-visit video service.

**Phase 1 exit criteria:** 2 quarterly reports published · 3+ tier-one media citations · 5+ mandates signed
· MVP platform live · first booking closed through OTHO's own funnel.

## 3. Section 2 — Digital Marketing Strategy

### 3.1 Why omnichannel — the real reason

The input document's rationale ("Google captures intent, Meta creates demand") is correct but shallow. The
actual justification is the **decision architecture of a ₹1–5 crore purchase in an oversupplied market**:

1. **The consideration cycle is 6–9 months, not 6–9 days.** 70–80% of bookings occur after the second,
   third or fourth interaction. A single-channel strategy cannot maintain presence across that window.
2. **The buying unit is 2–4 people.** Buyer, spouse, parents funding part of it, and for NRIs a proxy in
   India. Google reaches the researcher; Meta reaches the household; WhatsApp reaches the group chat where
   the decision actually gets made.
3. **In a glut, the bottleneck moves from discovery to validation.** With ~97,000 units available, buyers do
   not need help finding options — they need help *eliminating* them. Google intercepts the elimination
   query ("Kokapet traffic problems", "Project X review"). Meta and retargeting sustain presence while the
   buyer eliminates. **This is why OTHO's comparison and negatives content outperforms listing ads: it
   serves the actual job.**
4. **AI answer engines now sit upstream of both.** ~60% of Google searches end without a click; ~83% of
   AI-Overview searches produce none. If OTHO is not the *cited source*, it is invisible before the auction
   even runs. This is a third channel, and the input document omits it entirely.

The three channels map to three distinct jobs:

| Channel | Job | Primary metric |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Search** | Intercept active elimination and comparison intent | Cost per *qualified* lead |
| **Meta** | Build the consideration set; sustain presence across a 6–9 month cycle; reach the household | Cost per qualified lead; assisted-conversion share |
| **AI/answer engines (AEO)** | Be the source that gets quoted before the buyer ever reaches a SERP | Citation share on target queries |

### 3.2 Google Ads architecture

**Benchmarks to plan against** (verified, India 2026): avg real-estate CPC **₹200–380**; luxury keywords
**₹150–400**; **Hyderabad new-launch CPL ₹700–2,000**; luxury CPL **₹2,500–6,000**.

**Campaign structure — five tiers, hard-separated by budget and bid strategy:**

**Tier 1 — Project & brand capture (highest intent, lowest CPL)**
Exact and phrase match on every mandate project name, its variants and misspellings, plus `[project] price`,
`[project] floor plan`, `[project] possession`, `[project] review`.
On a mandate, OTHO *is* the official sales channel, so this is defensive and cheap. Expect CPL well under
₹700.

**Tier 2 — The elimination cluster** ← *the highest-leverage, least contested inventory*
Nobody bids on doubt. Everybody bids on desire.

```
[project name] review          [project name] problems        [project name] negatives
is [area] good to live         [area] traffic problem         [area] waterlogging
[area] vs [area]               [project] vs [project]         [project] possession delay
loading factor [project]       [area] pros and cons
```

These queries have **near-zero competition, very high intent, and low CPC** — and OTHO is the only advertiser
in the market with honest content to land them on. A developer cannot bid on "Project X problems" and land
on a page that answers it. **This is a structural, non-copyable advantage and it should be the first
campaign built.**

**Tier 3 — Micro-market intent**
`4 BHK Kokapet`, `luxury apartments Financial District`, `villas Tellapur`, `flats near ORR`, segmented by
budget band and configuration. Highest volume, highest competition, highest CPC. Land on micro-market hub
pages, never the homepage.

**Tier 4 — Analytics Dictionary / informational**
`what is loading factor`, `carpet area vs super built up`, `UDS calculation`, `RERA carpet area meaning`.
Very low CPC (₹15–50), no direct conversion — and that is fine. **This is the retargeting-pool builder and
the top of the trust funnel.** Judge it on cost-per-audience-addition, not CPL.

**Tier 5 — NRI geo campaigns**
US, UAE, UK, Singapore. `buy property Hyderabad from USA`, `NRI property investment Hyderabad`, `FEMA
property rules NRI`, `repatriation sale proceeds India`. Higher CPC, far higher ticket size, longer cycle.
Run on separate budgets with separate CPL targets — blending them with domestic campaigns will make both
sets of numbers meaningless.

**Cost mechanics — the Quality Score lever**

A high Quality Score buys a **20–40% cost advantage** on the identical keyword. OTHO's advantage here is
structural rather than tactical: a comparison engine result and a dictionary page are *genuinely* the most
relevant possible destinations for comparison and definitional queries. Developer microsites cannot match
that relevance because they only ever have one answer.

Operational rules:
- One landing page per ad group. Never route paid traffic to the homepage.
- Sub-2-second LCP on mobile. Most of this traffic is mobile, on Indian networks, mid-consideration.
- Negative keyword list built before launch: `rent`, `PG`, `hostel`, `jobs`, `salary`, `plots below 10 lakhs`,
  `free`, `owner direct`, `no brokerage`, `government scheme`, plus systematic mining weekly.
- Search-term review weekly for the first 8 weeks, fortnightly after.
- **Do not start on Performance Max.** It obscures the query data OTHO needs to learn the market. Run
  Search-only until the account has 90 days of clean conversion data, then test PMax as an incremental layer.

### 3.3 Meta Ads architecture

**Benchmarks:** India real estate CPL **₹400–900**; Tier-1 cities **₹550–1,400**; luxury **₹1,500+**. Lower
CPL than Google, materially lower intent — so Meta must be judged on **cost per qualified lead**, never raw
CPL. Optimising Meta on raw CPL is the most common and most expensive mistake in Indian real estate
marketing; it trains the algorithm to find people who click, not people who buy.

**Three-stage funnel:**

**TOF — Demand creation and authority**
- Micro-market documentaries (60–90s): *"We drove Kokapet to HITEC City at 9am. Here's the real number."*
- Honest Negatives clips — the highest organic-share content OTHO will produce, because it is genuinely
  counter-normative for the category.
- Prop-Talk episode cuts.
- Optimise for ThruPlay and qualified traffic. **Do not run lead-gen objectives at this stage.**

**MOF — Consideration and tool-led capture**
- Comparison carousels: two projects, side-by-side, real numbers — loading factor, true density, UDS,
  price per *carpet* foot.
- Lead magnets that qualify by their nature: the Loading Factor Calculator, the Inventory Report download,
  the NRI FEMA checklist. Someone who completes a UDS calculation is a demonstrably different prospect from
  someone who taps "Get Price."
- Instant Forms with **2–3 qualifying questions** (budget band, timeline, configuration). This raises CPL
  and raises lead quality far more. Accept the trade.

**BOF — Conversion**
- Dynamic retargeting keyed to the *specific* comparison or project page viewed, 30-day window.
- Site-visit offers, ready-to-move urgency, possession-timeline proximity.
- Exclusions: everyone already in CRM at qualified stage or beyond. Wasted retargeting spend on existing
  pipeline is pure margin leakage.

**Audiences**
- **IT professionals**: workplace and behavioural targeting around Gachibowli, HITEC City, Financial
  District, Kondapur; interest layers on the major GCC employers.
- **NRIs**: US, UAE, UK, Singapore, Australia — separate creative, separate landing pages, separate CPL
  targets. NRI creative should lead on *verification and FEMA safety*, not lifestyle. The remote buyer's
  dominant emotion is fear of being cheated at distance.
- **Lookalikes** seeded from closed-won CRM data (not from all leads — seed the model on buyers, not
  enquirers).
- **Life-event and behavioural**: recent job change, recently married, high-value-purchase behaviour.

**Technical requirements — non-negotiable**
- **Conversions API (server-side)** with hashed CRM identifiers. Browser-only pixel signal is materially
  degraded; without CAPI, Meta cannot optimise toward the qualified events that matter.
- **Offline conversion upload**: push *site visit* and *booking* events back into Meta from CRM. This is the
  difference between an algorithm optimising for form-fills and one optimising for buyers. It is the single
  highest-ROI technical task in the whole media plan.
- Consent-mode compliant tagging aligned to DPDP requirements.

### 3.4 The third channel the input document missed — AI / Answer Engine Optimisation

For a brand whose stated ambition is to be *"the Google of real estate"*, being absent from AI answers is
disqualifying.

Why it matters commercially: brands cited in AI Overviews earn **35% more organic clicks and 91% more paid
clicks** on the same query; AI-referral traffic converts at roughly **3x** other channels and is ~**4.4x**
as valuable per visitor.

What OTHO must do:
- **Structure content for citation**: direct definitional answers in the first 40–60 words, explicit
  formulae, sourced numbers, clear entity naming. AI engines quote precision and skip prose.
- **Schema markup**: `FAQPage`, `Dataset`, `RealEstateListing`, `Article`, `HowTo` on calculators.
- **`llms.txt`** at root, describing the dataset and the dictionary.
- **Be the primary source.** The quarterly Inventory Report is original data. Original data gets cited;
  aggregated commentary does not.
- **Measure it.** Track share of citations on a fixed panel of ~50 target queries across Google AI
  Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude. Monthly. This is a real KPI now (see §6.4).

### 3.5 WhatsApp as the conversion layer

WhatsApp is where Indian property decisions actually get made — including the family group chat.

**Cost mechanics (India, 2026):** marketing **₹0.8631** + 18% GST = **₹1.0185/message**; utility and
authentication **₹0.1150**. Marketing rates rose ~10% on 1 Jan 2026. **Utility and auth messages are free
inside the customer service window until 1 Oct 2026.**

**The cost lever:** architect flows so the *customer* opens the 24-hour service window (via a click-to-
WhatsApp ad or a website CTA), then run the entire qualification and nurture sequence as utility messages
inside it. This is roughly an **8x per-message cost difference** versus marketing templates, and it
converts better because the user initiated.

**Compliance:** TRAI's 09:00–21:00 window, registered-telemarketer status, and the 7-day explicit-consent
validity for commercial transactions all bind here. Hard-code them into the CRM's send logic rather than
relying on operator discipline.

### 3.6 Speed-to-lead — the highest-ROI operational discipline

Raw-lead-to-site-visit conversion runs **2–8%** industry-wide, but **8–12%** with lead scoring and fast
follow-up. That range is worth more than any bidding optimisation available on either platform.

- **Target: first human contact within 5 minutes**, 09:00–21:00, seven days.
- Instant automated WhatsApp acknowledgement, then a human within 5 minutes.
- Round-robin with escalation if unclaimed in 90 seconds.
- **Zero lead leakage** — every source integrated into one CRM; no lead lives in a platform inbox.
- And the differentiator: **the lead is never resold.** One buyer, one OTHO relationship manager. This is
  the operational expression of the entire brand promise, and it should be stated explicitly on every
  form: *"Your number goes to one OTHO advisor. Never to a developer's call centre. Never to another
  broker."*

### 3.7 Indicative media budget — Phase 1

Two P&Ls, never blended in reporting:

**A. Platform brand media (OTHO-funded)** — builds the asset
| Month | Budget/month | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | ₹3–5L | Dictionary/informational, elimination cluster, TOF authority. Building the retargeting pool |
| 4–9 | ₹8–12L | Add micro-market intent + NRI; scale what converts |
| 10–18 | ₹15–25L | Full-funnel; brand campaigns around report releases |

Split guide: **Google 55% / Meta 35% / AEO + content amplification 10%.** Google weighted higher than the

**B. Mandate media (developer-funded)** — deployed per project, governed by ROAS against that project's

### 4.3 Radical Transparency and Honest Negatives — how to actually ship it

This is the strategy's biggest differentiator **and its biggest execution risk**. The commercial tension is
real: OTHO earns commission from developers while publishing unflattering facts about their locations. It
needs structure, not courage.

**The Negatives Charter — publish this document publicly**

**1. Only three categories of negative are ever published:**

| Category | Example | Legal exposure |
|---|---|---|
| **Civic & environmental** — sourced to public record | "TG PCB station X recorded PM2.5 of N µg/m³, Nov–Jan average" · "GHMC recorded waterlogging complaints at this junction in the 2025 monsoon" | Low — factual, attributed, public record |
| **Computed structural** — arithmetic from filed documents | "Loading factor 41% vs the 25–35% norm" · "True density 118 units/acre vs a 94 units/acre micro-market median" · "UDS 18% below peer set" | Low — mathematics from TG-RERA filings |
| **Measured & timestamped** — OTHO's own audit | "Drive time to HITEC City: 52 min at 09:15 on a Tuesday, measured 12 Aug 2026" | Low — disclosed methodology, timestamped |

**2. Three categories are never published:** subjective construction-quality judgements · allegations about
developer character or intent · unverified third-party or forum claims.

The distinction is simple and it is the whole legal safety model: **"this project has a 41% loading factor
against a 25–35% norm"** is a defensible arithmetic statement. **"this builder cheats you on area"** is
litigable. OTHO publishes only the first kind — and the first kind is *more* persuasive to an analytical
buyer anyway.

**3. Right of reply, never right of veto.** Every developer gets 7 days' notice before a negative publishes
and an unedited response published alongside it. This is written into the mandate contract from Mandate #1
— so it is a known, agreed term rather than a fight discovered later.

**4. Negatives apply to OTHO's own mandates too.** Non-negotiable. The moment OTHO exempts its own
inventory, the entire asset is worthless — and buyers will notice within a quarter.

**5. Always pair a negative with mitigation.** *"Kokapet has peak-hour congestion on the ORR approach and
localised monsoon waterlogging on the internal roads. Towers on the eastern edge with the elevated approach
are materially less affected. If you commute before 08:30 or after 10:30, the delta is roughly 18 minutes."*
This is the difference between being useful and being merely negative — and it is what converts a
transparency reader into a lead.

**Selling it to developers — the argument that actually works:**

1. **Buyer's remorse is the enemy of both parties.** Cancellations and delayed registrations cost the
   developer more than an honest disclosure does.
2. **The negatives are already public** — on Reddit, on X, in society WhatsApp groups, in Google reviews.
   The choice is not "published vs unpublished." It is **"contextualised with mitigation, or uncontrolled."**
3. **Good projects win from disclosure.** A developer with a 28% loading factor and 88 units/acre gains
   enormously when those fields become standard comparison criteria. Structural transparency is a *weapon*
   for the well-built project. Lead the mandate pitch with this — it turns the objection into a selling point.
4. Empirically, disclosed-negative content drives longer session times and higher lead quality, because it
   filters out buyers who would have cancelled after the site visit.

## 5. Section 4 — Website Architecture & Inclusions

### 5.0 The architectural principle

**The website is a viewer for a dataset. The dataset is the product.**

The hard problem is not React components — it is acquiring, normalising, verifying and maintaining
structured data across TG-RERA filings, HMDA/GHMC sanction records, IGRS/Dharani registration data,
developer brochures and physical audit. **This is where PropTech companies fail, and it should absorb the
majority of early engineering effort.** Build the pipeline before the polish.

**Data pipeline (build in this order):**

1. **Ingestion** — TG-RERA registrations and quarterly progress filings; HMDA/GHMC approvals; IGRS market
   values and revised guidance values (statewide revision effective 5 June 2026); developer brochures and
   price sheets; OTHO's physical audit.
2. **Normalisation** — one canonical schema. Every area figure stored as **RERA carpet area** with SBA as a
   derived, clearly-labelled field. This one decision is the platform's entire integrity model.
3. **Verification** — human-in-the-loop. Every project carries a verification status and a last-verified
   date, both displayed publicly. *"Verified on 12 Aug 2026 by an OTHO analyst who visited the site"* is
   worth more than any badge.
4. **Computation** — loading factor, true density, UDS, price per carpet foot, possession-delay index, all
   derived automatically so they can never be selectively omitted.
5. **Serving** — API-first, so the same data powers the website, the comparison engine, the B2B product and
   the AI-citable schema markup.

**Stack:** Next.js (SSR/ISR for SEO — correct call in the input document) · PostgreSQL + PostGIS for
geospatial · headless CMS for editorial · LeadSquared or Erino for CRM (India-native, real-estate-specific,
WhatsApp-integrated) · Meta CAPI + Google Enhanced Conversions server-side · cloud-agnostic hosting.

Ship the **MVP with 50 deeply-verified projects**, not 5,000 shallow ones. Depth is the differentiator;
breadth is what the portals already have and it has not helped them.

### 5.1 The Granular Comparison Engine

Compare 2–4 projects across ~120 structured fields in eight groups:

| Group | Representative fields |
|---|---|
| **Regulatory** | TG-RERA number (linked to the live filing), approval status, sanctioned vs built plan variance, litigation flags, occupancy certificate status |
| **Space mathematics** | RERA carpet area, SBA, **loading factor**, **UDS (sq ft and %)**, balcony/utility area, **price per carpet foot vs price per SBA foot** |
| **Density & scale** | Total units, plot area, **true density (units/acre)**, units per lift/core, parking ratio, open-space ratio, clubhouse sq ft per unit |
| **Construction** | Structure type, seismic compliance, wall specification, flooring, fittings brand-by-brand, lift make and count, water source, STP/rainwater capacity |
| **Financial** | Base rate, floor-rise, amenity charges, corpus, maintenance ₹/sq ft/month, **total cost of ownership over 5 years**, stamp duty and registration |
| **Timeline** | RERA-committed possession vs current projected, construction-stage photographs with dates, **developer's historical delivery-delay record across prior projects** |
| **Location reality** | **Measured** drive times at peak and off-peak, metro/ORR proximity, schools, hospitals, water supply source, **Honest Negatives** |
| **Investment** | Rental yield estimate, comparable resale transactions from IGRS, appreciation history, absorption rate for that project |

**Design principles:**
- **Difference-first rendering.** Default to showing only fields where the projects materially differ.
  Hundreds of identical rows is data, not insight.
- **Every metric explains itself.** Inline "what is this / why it matters" linking to the Dictionary entry.
- **Every metric shows its source and date.**
- **A stated verdict.** *"For a 4-BHK end-user prioritising usable space, Project A delivers 312 more sq ft
  of carpet at a ₹340/sq-ft-carpet higher rate. For an investor prioritising land value, Project B's UDS is
  22% higher."* CarWale's actual lesson is not the comparison table — it is the **willingness to conclude**.
- Shareable comparison URLs (this is how the family group chat decision happens) and PDF export.

### 5.2 True Density

`True Density = Total Units ÷ Total Land Area (acres)`

But the headline number alone under-delivers. Publish the **congestion profile**:

| Derived metric | Formula | Why a buyer cares |
|---|---|---|
| Units per acre | units ÷ acres | Headline crowding |
| **Persons per acre** | units × avg household size (3.4) ÷ acres | The number people actually feel |
| **Units per lift** | units ÷ total lifts | Peak-hour lift wait — the most-complained-about daily friction in high-rises |
| **Parking ratio** | parking slots ÷ units | Visitor-parking conflict, the #1 source of society disputes |
| **Clubhouse sq ft per unit** | clubhouse area ÷ units | Whether the marketed 36,000 sq ft clubhouse is actually usable |
| **Open space per person** | open area ÷ (units × 3.4) | Genuine liveability |
| **Peak lift-wait estimate** | modelled from units/lift, floors, lift speed | The single most tangible density output |

Present against a micro-market median so the number has meaning: *"94 units/acre — 12% below the Kokapet
median of 107."* Add a plain-language band (Comfortable / Moderate / Dense / Very Dense) with the threshold
methodology published.

### 5.3 The Analytics Dictionary

The brand's central claim, rendered as product. Every entry: definition → formula → worked Hyderabad
example with real numbers → cost impact in rupees → interactive calculator → linked projects → an
"is this normal?" benchmark band.

**Priority entries:**

**RERA Carpet Area vs Super Built-Up Area.** RERA legally mandates carpet-area-based pricing and sale
agreements; SBA is the inflated marketing figure that adds a proportionate share of lobbies, lift shafts,
staircases and clubhouses. Because SBA is bigger, quoting price-per-SBA-foot makes a project look cheaper
than it is. **OTHO converts every quoted price to price-per-carpet-foot as the default display unit
platform-wide** — SBA shown only as a secondary, labelled field. This is the platform's most important
single design decision, because it makes genuine apples-to-apples comparison structurally unavoidable.

**Loading Factor.** `(SBA − Carpet) ÷ Carpet × 100`. Worked example: 1,000 sq ft carpet sold as 1,400 sq ft
SBA = **40% loading**. Benchmark honestly: **25–35% is normal**; large integrated townships with central
parks and extensive sports facilities can *legitimately* carry **35–45%** for genuinely shared amenities.
Publishing that nuance is what makes the metric credible rather than alarmist — and it protects OTHO from
the accusation that it is simply attacking high-loading projects.

**UDS (Undivided Share of Land).** `(Carpet Area of Your Flat ÷ Total Carpet Area of All Flats) × Total Plot
Area`. The structure depreciates over 20–30 years; the land appreciates. UDS determines resale value,
redevelopment compensation and ownership rights over shared spaces. **A large SBA with a low UDS is the
trap** — the buyer pays for concrete and marketing while owning very little land. Display UDS in absolute
sq ft, as a percentage, and as an implied land value per unit.

**Also required:** carpet vs built-up vs super built-up (all three) · floor-rise · PLC · corpus fund vs
maintenance · occupancy certificate vs completion certificate · encumbrance certificate · GST on
under-construction (5% without ITC; total acquisition cost typically 11–12% above base agreement value) ·
stamp duty and registration in Telangana · TDS on property purchase · possession vs registration ·
sanctioned plan vs brochure plan.

### 6.5 Attribution and measurement design

A 6–9 month consideration cycle breaks standard attribution. Design for it deliberately:

- **Cohort by first-touch month**, then track that cohort to booking. Monthly CPL/CPA snapshots are
  actively misleading in this category, and will cause the team to kill the campaigns that are working.
- **Offline conversion upload is mandatory** — push site visit and booking events from CRM back to Google
  and Meta. Without it, both platforms optimise toward form-fills rather than buyers, and the media budget
  quietly degrades.
- **Self-reported attribution** on every lead form ("How did you first hear about OTHO?"). It is imperfect
  and it is still the best available correction for the dark-social and podcast contribution that no pixel
  will ever capture.
- **Assisted-conversion reporting** for content, podcast and AI-referral channels — these will look
  worthless on last-click and are likely OTHO's highest-quality sources.
- **Incrementality testing** via geo holdouts once monthly spend exceeds ~₹10L.

### 6.6 Metrics to deliberately ignore

Naming these prevents the team optimising toward them:

| Vanity metric | Why it misleads |
|---|---|
| Raw lead volume | The classifieds model's core metric. Chasing it recreates exactly what OTHO exists to replace |
| Raw CPL alone | Meta will happily deliver ₹200 leads that never answer the phone |
| Total page views | Undifferentiated traffic; the dictionary drives volume, mandates drive revenue |
| Social followers | No causal relationship to bookings in this category |
| App downloads | Real estate is a low-frequency purchase; the mobile web is the right surface |
| GMV | The Square Yards trap: ₹1,410cr revenue, 3.3% EBITDA |

### 6.7 Phase gates — the discipline that prevents premature scaling

**Do not proceed to the next phase until every gate is met.**

**Gate 1 → Phase 1 (~Month 3):** RERA registered · 50 projects verified · legal sign-off on the negatives
charter · CRM live with no-resale architecture.

**Gate 2 → Phase 2 (~Month 18):** 10+ mandates live · Clearance Velocity ≤13 months · blended CAC ≤40% ·
contribution margin ≥55% · ≥40,000 organic sessions/month · NPS ≥60 · positive contribution on at least
three consecutive mandates.

**Gate 3 → pan-India (~Month 30):** Hyderabad contribution margin ≥60% · mandate renewal rate ≥75% · B2B
data ARR ≥₹3cr · a documented, transferable operating playbook · management depth sufficient to run
Hyderabad without founder involvement.

> **The most common failure mode for this business is expanding to Bengaluru at Month 20 because growth
> looks good, before Hyderabad's unit economics are proven. Gate 3 exists specifically to prevent that.**

## 7. Risk register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Developer refuses mandate over Honest Negatives | Medium | High | Negatives Charter in the contract from Mandate #1; right of reply not veto; lead the pitch with "structural transparency favours well-built projects" |
| Defamation claim | Low–Medium | High | Only public-record, computed, or timestamped-measured facts. Counsel sign-off on the standard. Never subjective quality claims |
| TG-RERA advertising violation | Medium | **Existential for a trust brand** | RERA number templated into every ad format; quarterly compliance audit; single named accountable owner |
| Data pipeline underestimated | **High** | High | Treat data as the primary engineering effort. Launch with 50 deep projects, not 5,000 shallow. Human-in-the-loop verification from day one |
| Market overhang extends past 24 months | Medium | High | Mandate mix shifted toward ready-to-move and resale; the advisory and data revenue lines are counter-cyclical |
| Incumbent copies comparison features | High | Medium | Moat is exclusive inventory + verified proprietary data + trust brand — not features. Portals cannot adopt "we never resell your number" without cannibalising their own P&L |
| Founder key-person dependency | High | High | Second-line advisory leadership by M12; Prop-Talk co-host by episode 10; document the mandate playbook |
| Working capital squeeze on back-ended commissions | Medium | High | Negotiate milestone-based commission release; developer-funded media so OTHO does not front campaign cost |
| Metro Phase 2 never gets financial sanction | Medium | Medium | The Confidence Index means OTHO never over-promised — this becomes a credibility *win*, not a liability |
| Advisory capacity constrains growth | High | Medium | Tooling that raises revenue-per-advisor; the platform must reduce advisory hours per transaction, not add to them |
